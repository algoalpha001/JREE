RESULTS OVERVIEW PAGE — REDESIGN IDEA
Reframe: from "here is your grade" to "here is what you've become."

CORE PRINCIPLE:
Nothing on this page should read as a report card. Every element should 
answer "what can I already do" before it answers "what's next." The 
score is a milestone marker on a path, not a verdict. Avoid literal 
clichés (no cartoon mountain climber, no confetti-burst-on-load, no 
trophy icons) — the feeling should come from motion, pacing, and 
language, not decoration.

THE ENTRY MOMENT (first 2-3 seconds matter most):
Before anything else loads, there's a brief holding beat — a single 
line of affirming copy appears first, alone, centered, THEN the score 
ring draws itself into place with a satisfying build-up motion (like 
a progress ring closing, Apple-Fitness-style), and the number counts 
up rather than appearing instantly. The percentile number should also 
count up in sync, slightly staggered after the score. This sequence 
— words first, then the numbers earning themselves — makes it feel 
like an unveiling of an achievement, not a data dump.

REFRAME THE HERO COPY:
Keep "You're interview-ready" as the emotional headline — that's 
already good, it names an identity/capability, not a grade. But 
strengthen the supporting line to lead with what they've achieved 
before mentioning comparison: something like "You think clearly, 
communicate well, and have strong fundamentals" BEFORE "you scored 
higher than 77% of candidates" — achievement first, comparison second, 
never the reverse.

THE PATH METAPHOR — DONE ABSTRACTLY, NOT LITERALLY:
Instead of the score ring floating alone, place it on a visual "journey 
line" — a subtle horizontal or gently ascending path element running 
behind/through the hero section, with small waypoint markers at 
band thresholds (C / B / A) along it, and the student's current 
position clearly marked as "further along" than most, not "not yet 
at the top." This visualizes progress-in-motion rather than a static 
grade box. The path should feel more like a route on a map or a 
trail marker system than a game health-bar — subtle, editorial, not 
gamified.

CELEBRATE STRENGTHS EXPLICITLY, DON'T JUST LIST NUMBERS:
The Layer Breakdown grid currently reads as 6 neutral score tiles. 
Instead: visually distinguish the strongest layer(s) with a small 
"your strongest skill" callout/tag directly on that tile — make the 
best-performing layer visually pop (slightly larger, subtle glow, or 
elevated) rather than all six tiles being equal-weight. The weakest 
layer should NOT be styled as a warning (no red, no alarm) — frame it 
with language like "biggest growth opportunity" and pair it with a 
one-line forward-looking note, not just a lower number sitting there 
unexplained.

ROLE FITS AS "DOORS ALREADY OPEN":
Reframe the role-fit bars from "here's your match %" to feel like 
unlocked opportunities — the top role fit (91%) should have a distinct 
"unlocked" or "top match" treatment (a badge, a highlight state) 
that visually differs from the ones below it, so the student's eye 
goes first to "this door is open to you" rather than scanning a 
ranked list of percentages.

MICRO-INTERACTIONS THAT REINFORCE CONFIDENCE:
— Hovering the score ring or overall score card could reveal a 
  one-line encouraging insight ("You're ahead of most Computer Science 
  graduates this year") rather than being a static, inert number.
— The percentile "77th" should feel like a highlight moment, not just 
  a stat — treat it with more visual presence than a plain oversized 
  number; consider a brief celebratory pulse/glow on load, once, 
  understated.
— Tab transitions (Overview → Score Breakdown → Role Readiness → 
  Improvement Plan → Certificate) should feel like moving forward in 
  a story, not switching data views — content should slide/fade in 
  progressively, and each tab's opening line should also lead with 
  affirming framing before diving into details.

IMPROVEMENT PLAN TAB SPECIFICALLY:
This tab is the biggest risk of feeling like a "here's what's wrong 
with you" list. Reframe entirely as "your next unlocks" — structure 
each improvement item as a short milestone card: current strength → 
specific next action → what it unlocks (a role, a band, a percentile 
jump). Never present a weak layer without immediately pairing it with 
a concrete, small, achievable next step. The tone throughout should 
read like a coach pointing at what's next, not an auditor pointing at 
what's missing.

BOTTOM ACTION ROW:
Keep "Download certificate" as the dominant action — but treat this 
moment as the natural payoff of the celebration, not just a utility 
button. Consider the certificate download/share actions appearing 
with a slight delay/entrance after the score reveal animation completes, 
so it feels like "now that you've seen what you've achieved, here's 
how you show it off" rather than being available immediately alongside 
everything else.

OVERALL FEELING TO ACHIEVE:
A student with Band B, 73/100 should close this page feeling like 
they just found out they're further along than they realized — proud, 
motivated, and clear on the next small step — not like they got their 
exam marks back. Every number on this page must be wrapped in language 
and motion that answers "what does this make me capable of" before it 
answers "how do I compare."
