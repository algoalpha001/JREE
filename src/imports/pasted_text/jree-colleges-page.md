Design prompt — JREE "For Colleges" page
Who this is for: Placement officers, TPOs, and college management — institutional buyers, not individuals. The placement officer has to justify this spend to a principal, so the page must make the ROI legible and the outcomes concrete. Their live worry is threefold: will students actually take it, will employers actually use it, what does the college get? Every section answers one of those. Tone is professional and outcome-focused — credible, not promotional. This is a procurement decision dressed as a landing page; polish and restraint read as "real institutional product."
Page-wide anti-slop guardrails — do NOT use:

three identical rounded feature cards, stock "students in a computer lab," glowing stat counters, gradient mesh / glassmorphism, emoji icons, "Empower your institution" / "Transform outcomes" copy, fake dashboard screenshots stuffed with chart noise, neon CTA pills. If a section could sit unchanged on any EdTech template, redesign it.
Page system (what makes it feel senior):

One disciplined grid down the whole page; sections vary in rhythm but share margins, type scale, and baseline.
Two weights only; type carries hierarchy. Numbers and headlines get weight, everything else stays calm.
Hairline rules over boxes wherever possible. Generous whitespace.
One consistent treatment for any imagery / dashboard shots so nothing reads as stock.
Sentence case throughout; small mono eyebrows only.


Section 1 — Hero

Eyebrow: For placement officers & college management.
Headline, large and flat: Give every student a placement score before placement season begins.
Sub: JREE assesses your entire batch and gives each student a verified score employers trust — and gives you a real-time dashboard of exactly where your batch stands.
Primary CTA: Request a demo. Secondary, quiet: Download sample report →
Hero device (not a stock photo): a batch dashboard glimpse — a cohort distribution across bands with a national-benchmark line cutting through it. The institutional answer to "where does my batch stand," shown not described. This is the college equivalent of the employer's candidate card.
Stat strip beneath, plainly stated, hairline-separated: ₹3,000 per student / year · 90-minute assessment · Scores shared with 50+ employers on JREE.

Section 2 — The problem (institutional empathy)

Eyebrow: The placement gap. Headline: Your students are capable. Employers just can't see it yet.
Body names the real injustice: capable graduates missing jobs for lack of a credible, standard proof; drives that favour good interviewers over good performers.
Treatment: the three pains as a short down-the-page sequence, hairline-separated — anecdotal data, employers skipping non-IIT/NIT colleges, no objective benchmark — building to the turn: an objective score lets a college prove it's better than its reputation suggests. Avoid a tidy 3-card row; the sequential read carries more weight.

Section 3 — How it works (effortless setup)

Eyebrow: Simple to set up. Powerful in practice. Headline: From contract to scores in 48 hours.
Treatment — a clean 5-step timeline (vertical desktop, stacked mobile), each step one icon + short label, connected by a thin through-line: EduBridge signs → upload CSV → students auto-emailed credentials → students take JREE in your window (90 min, any device) → live dashboard. Keep it deliberately operational — the job here is to make setup feel effortless and real, removing the "this will be a hassle" objection. Sparse labels; detail stays out of the steps.

Section 4 — What your college gets (justify the price)

Eyebrow: More than an assessment. Headline: A complete placement intelligence system.
Six benefits: batch analytics dashboard · national rank for every student · employer-pool visibility · AI batch insight · gap map · year-over-year tracking.
Treatment: lead with national rank — give it the most visual weight (it's the strongest hook; it's how a college outranks its brand). The rest as a restrained labelled list or unequal quiet cards — one icon + name + one tangible line each, no repeated chip kit, no glow. This section earns the ₹3,000, so each benefit must read as a concrete deliverable, not a feature word.

Section 5 — What your students get (reassure the "will they take it")

Eyebrow: For your students. Headline: A verified credential they carry for life.
Body: composite score, national rank, layer breakdown, role-readiness for 8+ roles; Band A/B visible to employers.
Treatment: quieter, warmer than the institutional sections — this is the human beat. Student benefits as a calm list: free to take · know where they stand nationally · employers come to them · AI interview feedback · shareable certificate. Frame gaps as what to improve before placement season, never as "weak."

Section 6 — Pricing (ROI made undeniable)

Eyebrow: Straightforward pricing. Headline: ₹3,000 per student per year.
Body: one fee covers everything; minimum 50 students; 500+ discounts.
Treatment: a single clean inclusion checklist (5-layer assessment · individual reports + rank · college dashboard · employer-pool listing · AI batch insight · exam-window control · EduBridge support) — not three pricing tiers; this is one transparent price, present it as such.
Lead the ROI line as the hero of this section, set apart: A batch of 200 at ₹3,000 = ₹6,00,000 — less than two campus drives, and it gives employers a reason to come to you. That sentence is what the TPO repeats to the principal; design it to be quotable.
CTAs: Get a quote for your college · Talk to our sales team.

Section 7 — Trust / credibility

Eyebrow: Why EduBridge? Headline: We've been placing students for 10 years. JREE is the product of that experience.
Body: 10,000+ placed across 200+ companies; built on real employer conversations.
Treatment — trust signals as plain fact, not badge soup: O*NET / EQF / WorldSkills frameworks · GST-verified employers only · DPDP-compliant · reviewed by IT/BFSI/Manufacturing experts. A quiet credibility strip. Leave clearly-marked slots for college/employer logos if any exist — real logos outperform any styling here.

Section 8 — Final CTA (lead capture)

Headline: Give your batch the edge they deserve. Sub: Registration is managed by the EduBridge sales team — fill the form and we'll reach out within 24 hours.
Treatment — a short form section, 5 fields max: name · college name · designation · phone · batch size. Full-width, calm, decisive. Primary: Request a callback. Secondary: Download the college brochure →.
Note: per the action rules, the form is built to capture and submit on the user's action — design the submit state clearly, but the page only sends on the officer's own click.


Locked rules (whole page):

No red — band/gap visuals use neutral + amber, never alarm; the gap map frames weakness as where to focus training, never a verdict on students.
Rank and benchmark framed as standing the college can prove and improve, never as ranking colleges against each other punitively.
Bespoke JREE token system; ignore the old HTML prototype styling.
Keep placeholder logos/brochure links as clearly-marked slots until real assets land.

Motion: minimal and purposeful — the batch distribution settling once, the 5-step timeline drawing in on scroll, the national-benchmark line dropping in last. Nothing loops or spins. Stillness reads as institutional credibility.
